Since its beginnings, Primavera Hacker has been an open space for people from all backgrounds, identities, and paths, who contribute diverse ideas and perspectives. We believe this plurality enriches the community: it brings new topics, broadens our knowledge, and strengthens our networks of collaboration.

We start from hacker culture as a creative, political, and ethical practice that defends freedom of information, free knowledge, free software and hardware, privacy, sustainability, mutual support, and the struggle against abuse of power. Hacking is not just programming or soldering: it is about creating tools, processes, and communities for a freer, more supportive society without oppressive hierarchies.
But this openness is not infinite. It has clear limits:

- There is no place in Primavera Hacker for fascism, xenophobia, racism, sexism, LGBT+phobia, ableism, or any other form of discrimination, oppression, or hate speech.

- We do not accept the promotion or justification of authoritarian ideologies, far-right extremism, or practices that seek to exclude, exploit, or degrade people or communities.

- We do not tolerate harassment, intimidation, or humiliation, whether physical, verbal, or digital, including behaviors such as offensive comments, non-consensual physical contact, unsolicited sexual material, hostile interruptions, stalking, or following.

Recent history reminds us that the resurgence of fascisms and organized exclusion is a real threat. In the face of this, we reaffirm that the hacker culture we defend is not neutral: it is committed to freedom, diversity, autonomy, and justice.

### Information Privacy

We invoke the Chatham House Rule, which states that participants have the right to use the information they receive, but the identity or affiliation of the speaker, or of any other participant, must not be revealed.
If you want to record or document the activity, you must first approach the organizers to ask for authorization, and you must respect the decision of a participant who does not wish to be photographed if they indicate so. Do not tweet, post, or publish information about individuals unless you have obtained their consent. This includes tagging them on social media.
