Primavera Hacker began in 2013 as an initiative of the collective **Hackería**, conceived as a para-academic activity sponsored by the Design Department of the Faculty of Architecture and Urbanism at the University of Chile (FAU), and with the support of the National Council for Culture and the Arts (CNCA) to facilitate the participation of international guests. Over time, and once the collective ceased to function as such, the festival continued bringing together different people, collectives, and organizations that gradually joined to shape each new edition.

Starting in 2016, the gathering focused on issues of digital activism and security for social organizations, building collaborations with initiatives such as **The Tor Project** and **Fundación Datos Protegidos**. In 2017, it had the support of **Derechos Digitales**, which managed the participation of numerous collectives and individuals from different Latin American countries, including Mexico, Argentina, and Brazil. After seven years of recess, in 2025 it re-emerges in collaboration with **Hacklab RLAB** of Buenos Aires and with the support of the **Chaos Computer Club** of Germany.

This year, under the motto **Chronically Online**, we ask about the digital present as an unavoidable condition—not pathological, but structured against us. We talk about cognitive warfare, affective loops, and the need to reclaim desire, infrastructure, and narrative.

## **Thematic Axes**

Primavera Hacker organizes its contents around curatorial axes. 

### **Computational Art and Aesthetics**

We seek works, actions, or reflections that use technologies to alter sensibility, produce dissonance, or expand language. Installations, glitches, texts, demos. Strictly no conceptual performances allowed.

### **Politics, Ethics, and Accelerationism**

The network is reality. What do we do when digital infrastructure becomes a device of affective and political control? We want accounts that address legislation, surveillance, platforms, borders, affects, surveillance capitalism, and digital disobedience.

### **Hardware, Tactics, and Materiality**

Everything that makes the digital world land in the physical world. Device hacks, open boards, recycled machines, intervened sensors. We are interested in both design and repair.

### **Science and Knowledge**

We share a deep distrust of technosolutionist salvation narratives. This axis seeks research—formal or not—that provides critical frameworks for thinking about crisis. We also believe it is important to stop demonizing the materialism of science. The fact that not all knowledge is scientific does not mean that scientific knowledge is unnecessary, nor that we cannot use it strategically.

### **Hacking and Offensive Security**

Talks that explore digital security from multiple fronts: from offensive analysis of protocols, binaries, applications, or embedded systems, to tools and methodologies linked to operations, threat intelligence, or frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK. We also welcome proposals focused on encryption, privacy, digital self-defense pedagogies, and technologies designed to protect at-risk communities. We are interested both in exploit-oriented research and in building collective capacities to resist and survive in hostile environments.
