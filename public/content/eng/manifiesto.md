The network is reality. AFK is no longer an escape. The fantasy of "real life" as an intact, pure space was a lie long before it became a marketing strategy. The logistics of perception have already been mapped, indexed, and monetized.

Being Chronically Online is not a pathology: it is the inevitable result of a system that needs you more than you need it. The problem is not being online, but how they have structured that condition against you.

Hyperconnectivity has ceased to be merely a technical condition and has become a field of dispute. The informational space has been reconfigured as an environment where perception, emotion, and behavior are intervened with in real time: cognitive warfare. The brain is not just an interface; it has become critical infrastructure, a target for operations designed to install cycles of anxiety, affective loops, and narratives of powerlessness.

Doomscrolling is not a passive act but a function, a process, a procedural form of participation where interaction is indistinguishable from despair. It is the way the algorithm metabolizes collapse into indexed reaction, refining disaster into predictable behavior. It ensures that the impossibility of action is experienced as an individual failure, that the weight of planetary collapse is absorbed by your attention and not by the infrastructures that cause it. Doomscrolling is not just anxiety or news: it is a process of sustained exposure that alters neurological rhythms and conditions of agency. A machinery of attrition with concrete effects.

It is urgent to distinguish between being connected and being swept away. The first implies intention, the second is capture. When we talk about inhabiting the online, we talk about active navigation, about links between nodes that do not respond to the protocol of the feed. No critique of isolation is valid if it does not consider the need to remake the common. It is not enough to reconfigure systems; we must also reconfigure bodies. The return to the physical is not nostalgia, but interference. Shared, synchronous, unpredictable spaces, capable of breaking the algorithmic regime of infinite affinity.

Disconnecting is not resistance; it is a managed absence, an illusion of autonomy that does not alter the underlying architecture. The escape hatch leads nowhere. Logging out is just another way of keeping the system intact, of delegating the problem to those still trapped inside. There is no purity in retreat, only a tactical repositioning within the same terrain. The feed continues with or without you.

The network is not the platform. The platform is not the infrastructure. The infrastructure is not inevitable. What comes next is not a return to a pre-platform internet; there is no lost utopia to recover. What comes next is an architecture built not around capture, but around navigation. It is not about being less online, but about being less extractable. Not about being invisible, but about being illegible to the systems that need legibility to function.

Chronically Online is not a state of being, it is a set of imposed conditions. It is not a personal failure, it is an inevitability of designed psychosis and hallucinations. The response is neither surrender nor detox. It is a calculated reconfiguration of the interface, an adjustment of perception that renders the feed useless as a control mechanism. The goal is not to abandon the network, but to make the network ungovernable.
